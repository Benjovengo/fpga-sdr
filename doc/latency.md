# Where the latency in a DDC comes from, and how this design reduces it

End-to-end latency of a decimating receiver chain has two very different parts:

| Part | Cause | Unit | Typical size here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structural** | pipeline registers inserted to meet timing | clock cycles | ~3 to 10 cycles |
| **Algorithmic** | the filters' group delay, i.e. how long the impulse response takes to "arrive" | output samples × decimation | 10s to 100s of cycles |

After decimation by *R*, every output sample of group delay costs *R* input clock cycles. With
R = 8, the 15-sample group delay of a 31-tap linear-phase FIR alone is 120 cycles, far more than
all the pipeline registers in the design. **So the biggest latency win is algorithmic, not
removing registers.**

## Technique 1: minimum-phase channel filter (algorithmic)

A symmetric (linear-phase) FIR delays every frequency by exactly (N-1)/2 samples. That property
matters when waveform shape must be preserved across a wide band (e.g. radar pulse
compression), but for many receivers (control loops, FM/AM audio, narrowband channels, feedback
and detection paths) only the magnitude response is specified.

A minimum-phase filter with **the same magnitude response** puts its energy at the start of the
impulse response, so its group delay at DC drops from 15 samples to a few. The design flow in
`model/design.py`:

1. Design the linear-phase filter `h` (CIC droop compensation + channel selection).
2. Convolve it with itself: `h * h` is still linear-phase with magnitude |H|².
3. Homomorphic (cepstral) minimum-phase conversion halves the log-magnitude, giving back |H|
   with minimum phase and the same 31 taps.
4. Normalize the DC gain, quantize to Q1.17.

Trade-off: the group delay is no longer constant across the passband (see
`images/group_delay.png`), so it adds phase distortion, and the step response overshoots
more (about 17 % vs. about 8 %, see `images/step_response.png`). Whether that's
acceptable is a system decision, which is why the RTL keeps both options behind a generic.

## Technique 2: transposed-form FIR (structural)

The direct form with a pipelined adder tree registers the delay line, every multiply, every
adder level and the output: 3 + ⌈log2(31)⌉ = 8 cycles from input to output.

The transposed form multiplies the *incoming* sample by all coefficients at once and accumulates
into a chain of partial-sum registers, so the output is ready **one cycle** after the input,
independent of the number of taps. It still runs fast because each tap is a single
multiply-add, which is exactly what a DSP48 slice implements (`P = A*B + C`, with the partial
sum in the P register). The cost is a high fan-out on the input sample, which matters at very
large tap counts (it can be fixed with a short input register tree, or a systolic form).

## Technique 3: no buffering, sample-by-sample streaming

Nothing in the chain waits for a block of samples: no FFT frames, no FIFOs in the data path, no
back-pressure handshakes (a radio front-end can't be stalled anyway). Each block forwards a
sample as soon as it exists, using a valid strobe.

## What is *not* done, on purpose

* **Removing pipeline registers in the NCO/mixer.** They cost 3 cycles out of 100+ and are
  what lets the design close timing at ~250 MHz on a Zynq-7000. Latency work starts with the
  biggest contributor.
* **Lowering the decimation or the CIC order.** That reduces latency too, but changes the
  receiver's selectivity and output rate, i.e. it's a different spec, not an optimization.

## How latency is measured

`tb/tb_ddc.vhd`, test `latency_step`: the NCO is set to 0 Hz and a DC step is applied to I.
The testbench counts clock edges from the edge that captures the first non-zero input sample to:

* **first response**: the first output-register update with a non-zero value, and
* **50 % of step**: the first output reaching half of its final value. This is the practical
  definition of "how long until the output reflects the input", including group delay.

Both are reported for all four configurations in [results.md](results.md), generated by
`run.py` on every run, including in CI.
